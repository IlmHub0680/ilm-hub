Model 7 — Course Specifications & Syllabi. Models 1–6 are the fixed academic foundation here — this model does not redesign departments, programs, prerequisites, or the course catalogue. It takes each catalogued course and adds the syllabus-level detail Model 6 deliberately didn't attempt: weekly sequencing, measurable learning outcomes, teaching methodology, assessment design, and a CLO → Teaching → Assessment → Evidence map. Where Model 6 already defined a field correctly (code, title, department, program, pathway, level, units, hours, prerequisites, type), this model carries it forward rather than re-deriving it. Because 42 courses is too much for one response, this model is built in batches — see §4 for how batches are sequenced and reported.

1. Scope & Constraints

  • This model does not change department structure, programs, prerequisites, or course codes. Where writing an actual syllabus surfaces a real inconsistency Models 1–6 didn't catch, it is flagged as a decision (§6) — never silently fixed here.
  • This model does not invent religious rulings, a fiqh madhab position, or a qira'ah/riwayah choice. Where a course's content depends on one of those, this model identifies the dependency and routes it to the Scholarly Review Committee (Model 2 §6) rather than deciding it — see §2's Islamic Scholarly Review framework and each course's own note.
  • Every batch ends with a status report — completed courses, remaining courses, issues discovered, decisions required — matching the brief's output rule exactly (§4).

2. Institution-Wide Conventions

Stated once here and referenced by every course below, rather than repeated per course — all provisional pending founder confirmation.

  • Term length. A 15-week term for every course, regardless of unit count. Weekly contact hours = Units (a 3-unit course runs 3 hours/week; a 1-unit course runs 1 hour/week) — the natural extension of Model 6 §3's "1 unit = 15 teaching hours," itself still pending Model 4 decision 22. A course may instead run as a shorter, front-loaded intensive within the same 15-week term when that serves the content better (RL-101 does this — see §5) — the exception is justified per course, not applied by default.
  • Expected learner workload. Teaching hours (contact) plus self-study hours. Self-study ≈ contact hours × 1 for most courses, × 1.5 for Recitation/Hifz and Language courses (practice-heavy), × 2 for Research-type courses (independent work) — illustrative, tied to the same open credit-hour decision as term length.
  • Assessment weighting, by course type:
    Course typeQuizzesAssignmentsMidtermPracticalFinalPassing requirement
    Core / Language / Research15%20%25%—40%50% overall, no component below 40%
    Recitation/Hifz10%10%20%30%30%50% overall AND minimum 60% on the combined Practical + Final recitation component
    Character/practical (adab, teaching practice)10%25%20%25%20%50% overall; the Practical component must show consistent demonstration over the term, not just a numeric average — instructor-attested, not purely computed
    Foundation-tier weighting deliberately favors ongoing/practical work over a single final exam, matching Model 3 §3's "competence-based, not exam-heavy" assessment expectation for this pathway.
  • Academic integrity. Written work must be the learner's own; recitation and practical assessments must be the learner's own unaided performance; unauthorized assistance on individually-assessed quizzes or exams is not permitted. Suspected violations are reported to the Examinations Officer through the existing Request system (Model 2 §2); a first violation typically means resubmission or a grade penalty at instructor discretion, a repeat or severe violation escalates to the Head of Department and may engage the Academic Standing process (the existing AcademicStanding enum). Proposed, not yet founder-confirmed.
  • Course review cadence. Every course is reviewed by its Head of Department at least once per academic year, on the same cycle as the Assessment & Quality Assurance Unit's QualityReview mechanism (Model 2 §5). A course specification is revised whenever its department identifies a needed change, using the same update discipline applied across Models 1–6.
  • Instructor requirements, baseline. Subject-matter competence at or above the tier being taught, appointed via Head of Department → Dean (Model 2 §7). Content bearing on Islamic rulings or positions requires Scholarly Review Committee clearance before use. Each course below states only what's genuinely course-specific beyond this baseline.
  • Islamic Scholarly Review, framework. Per Model 1 §10 and Model 2 §6, any content bearing on Islamic rulings, positions, or interpretation is reviewed and approved by the Scholarly Review Committee before being taught under the Academy's name. This model identifies where that review is needed per course — it does not supply the ruling, madhab position, or qira'ah/riwayah choice itself, all of which remain open Founder or Committee decisions. Course content consistent with the Academy's already-confirmed manhaj (Ahlus-Sunnah wal-Jama'ah, upon the understanding of the Salaf — Model 1 §12 decision 1) is treated as safe to outline at a topic level; content depending on the still-open madhab or riwayah decisions is flagged, not resolved.
  • Required texts. Drawn from the Academy's own Bookstore catalogue where a genuine topical match exists, rather than inventing new titles. Where no bookstore title fits, this model says so plainly instead of filling the gap with an invented source.
