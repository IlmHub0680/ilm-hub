5. Batch 7 — Diploma: Islamic Education & Tarbiyah (Teaching Track)

The Diploma teaching track's four required-specialization companions (IE-401, IE-403, IE-404, IE-405, all branching from IE-101) plus IE-402 (Da'wah & Outreach), whose tier, prerequisite, and track placement remain genuinely open pending decision 32 (§7) — restated here rather than resolved, per the "flag inconsistencies for approval" rule (this brief's opening instruction). IE-401, IE-402, IE-404, and IE-405 are observed/practiced-skill courses and use the Character/practical profile (§2), the same profile already applied to IE-201/IE-202 despite Model 6 catalogue "type" labels not distinguishing this; IE-403 (Curriculum Design) is primarily written-design work and uses the Core/Language/Research profile instead. This batch also carries forward, rather than resolves, Model 6's audited gap that no "Practical" (practicum/placement) course exists anywhere in the catalogue (§8) — a teaching practicum would naturally sit here, alongside IE-401, but adding one is a catalogue-level change outside this brief's scope and is flagged again below rather than invented.

IE-401 — Islamic Education & Teaching Methodology

PrerequisiteIE-101 (Islamic Education Foundations)
Course typeRequired specialization (teaching track)
Passing requirement50% overall, Practical+Final combined minimum 60% (Character/practical profile, §2)

Description. How to teach Islamic content effectively: lesson planning, classroom management, and instructional methods suited to an Islamic-education setting — the teaching track's methodological foundation.

Objectives. Equip learners to plan and deliver a well-structured lesson on Islamic content and manage a learning environment appropriately.

Learning Outcomes. By course end, learners will be able to: (1) explain core pedagogical principles relevant to Islamic education (learner engagement, scaffolding, assessment for learning); (2) produce a complete lesson plan for a given Islamic-content topic; (3) demonstrate a short microteaching segment applying that plan; (4) apply basic classroom-management techniques in a simulated scenario; (5) evaluate a peer's microteaching for pedagogical effectiveness, constructively.
