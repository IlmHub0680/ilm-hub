Model 5 — Department & Program Curriculum Design. Models 1–4 are fixed foundations here, not renegotiated. This model answers a question Model 4 deliberately left open: not every program should have the same courses, and not every course belongs to the department someone might guess first. It gives each of the Academy's five departments a defined topic list, resolves every place two departments' topics appeared to overlap, and corrects two course attributions Models 3 and 4 got wrong before this model existed to catch them. No weekly syllabi, and no final course catalogue, yet.

1. Departmental Ownership Principle

Every course belongs to exactly one department. That department designs it, sequences it, and staffs it (Model 2 §3); a program may draw courses from several departments, but a course itself is never co-owned. Model 4 §1 already established that a program draws only the departments its purpose needs — Foundation Studies does not need Islamic Civilization & Society, the Diploma does. This model adds the other half of that rule: when two departments' subject matter appears to overlap, the overlap must be resolved — by scope, by reassignment, or by folding one into the other — before any course for it is created. An unresolved overlap is not a minor gap; it is exactly how a catalogue ends up with two courses teaching the same thing under two different names, which Model 1's Institutional Objectives and Model 2's Academic Design Rule both exist to prevent.

Two corrections this model makes to already-published work. While reconciling department topic lists, this model found that Model 4's ADV-CS-301 ("Islamic Thought") and DIP-CS-401 ("Contemporary Muslim Issues & Da'wah") had drifted into territory that belongs to Islamic Studies and Islamic Education & Tarbiyah respectively. Per the standing rule to update earlier models rather than let them go stale, ADV-CS-301 is renamed Islamic Social Thought, DIP-CS-401 is renamed Contemporary Muslim Issues (its da'wah component moved to Islamic Education & Tarbiyah), and Model 2 §3's department descriptions and Model 3 §7's certificate table have been updated to match. See §9 for the full reasoning.

8. Specialization Framework

  • One department per certificate. A Specialized Certificate belongs to exactly one department, matching Model 3 §7's example table (now corrected for Da'wah — see §1). No certificate may combine content from two departments; that would recreate the exact ambiguity §9 exists to resolve.
  • The Advanced elective previews a real track. The Advanced pathway's single specialization elective (ADV-SPEC-30x, Model 4 §6) is the formal preview mechanism — whichever certificate track a learner samples there should draw from the same department the eventual certificate belongs to, so the preview is genuine rather than decorative.
  • Certificates draw only from their department's own courses. Model 4 §2 defines a certificate as three to five focused courses in one discipline — meaning the gap-filling courses identified in §§2–6 above are also the future raw material for new certificates (a future Da'wah certificate, for instance, would draw on the new Da'wah & Outreach course once it exists as a real Course record).

9. Course Duplication Audit

Five ways an apparent overlap between two departments' topics can be resolved, applied to every overlap this model found:

  1. Differentiate — keep both, split by scope, rename if the names alone invite confusion.
  2. Reassign — move the topic to a single owning department entirely.
  3. Fold in — treat it as content within an existing course; no new course created.
  4. Merge — combine two proposed gap topics into one new course.
  5. No duplication — confirm the apparent overlap doesn't hold once scope is checked.
OverlapResolutionReasoning
Islamic Thought (Islamic Studies, new) vs. the course formerly named "Islamic Thought" (Islamic Civilization & Society)Differentiate + renameIslamic Studies' version is the classical intellectual/theological tradition; the Civilization & Society course is its civilizational and social application. Renamed to Islamic Social Thought (§1) so the two names no longer collide.
Da'wah (outreach) methodology, previously under Islamic Civilization & SocietyReassignDa'wah is fundamentally a pedagogical and outreach skill — teaching and conveying — not a historical or civilizational study area. Moved to Islamic Education & Tarbiyah (§1, §5), which already owns pedagogy.
Contemporary Islamic Issues (Islamic Studies, new) vs. Contemporary Muslim Issues (Islamic Civilization & Society)DifferentiateIslamic Studies' version asks the juristic "can/should" question (fiqh and aqeedah reasoning on bioethics, finance, technology); Civilization & Society's version asks the sociological "what's happening, and how does the community respond" question. Different disciplines answering different questions — kept as two courses.

Spinning them out would create near-duplicate courses purely to look thorough — exactly what Model 2's Academic Design Rule warns against.

11. Decisions Requiring Approval

Continuing the numbering from Models 1–4.

  1. Da'wah & Outreach's placement — open. Should it become a third Diploma elective alongside the teaching-track and community-track electives, fold into the existing teaching-track elective (DIP-ED-401), or wait for a future Specialized Certificate? This model identifies the course and its department; it does not decide where it sits in the Diploma's structure.
  2. New course tier placements — open. The tiers and sequencing in §10 are illustrative, not confirmed — the same review Model 4's original study-plan tables are still awaiting (Model 4 decisions 22–23) applies here too.
  3. Seerah's missing Advanced/Diploma continuation — open. Flagged in §2. Is this deliberate, the same way Tazkiyah is assumed after Intermediate, or a genuine gap needing its own course? Needs a founder call, not an assumption.
  4. Family Education vs. Family & Society — confirm the split. §9 resolves this by scope, but the names are close enough to confuse in practice; worth explicit founder sign-off before either course is built.
