Model 6 — Course Catalogue & Coding System. Models 1–5 are fixed foundations here, not renegotiated. This model turns every course named across Models 4 and 5 into one master catalogue: a permanent coding system (closing Model 4 decision 23), a numbering framework that encodes academic level without guesswork, full course-level detail for all 42 courses identified so far, and the quality-control and gap audits a real catalogue needs before any of it becomes actual Course records. Codes in Models 4 and 5 have been updated to match this model throughout. No weekly syllabi yet.

1. Coding System

Every code follows [DEPT]-[LEVEL][SEQ] — a two-letter department code, a one-digit level, and a two-digit sequence number within that department and level.

This finalizes and replaces Model 4's temporary [PATHWAY]-[DEPT]-[SEQ] placeholders (decision 23). It keeps Model 4's original numbers unchanged and simply drops the redundant pathway-name prefix, since the level digit already carries that information — see §2. The one substantive change is department lettering: Islamic Education & Tarbiyah moves from Model 4's ad hoc "ED" to "IE", and Islamic Civilization & Society from "CS" to "IC", both closer to the department's actual name. Who assigns new codes: proposed as the Registrar's responsibility going forward, matching Model 4 decision 23's original framing — open for your confirmation (decision 31, §10).

2. Course Numbering Framework

The level digit is not decorative — it is the rule, applied without exception across all 42 courses below:

DigitLevel

The sequence digits (the last two) simply order courses within a department and level — IS-301, IS-302, IS-303, IS-304 carry no meaning individually beyond "the 1st through 4th Islamic Studies courses at Advanced." One exception: the Advanced pathway's single specialization elective keeps a floating, department-agnostic placeholder — SPEC-3xx — because which department it eventually draws from varies by learner choice (Model 4 §6); it is not itself a numbered course.

3. Master Course Catalogue

All 42 courses identified across Models 4 and 5, grouped by owning department — grouping by department here serves as both the master catalogue and the department course lists Model 6 §6 asks for. Each department's quick-reference table is followed by every course's description, primary learning outcome, and assessment type. Teaching hours use an illustrative 1 unit = 15 teaching hours conversion — not a confirmed credit system, still pending Model 4 decision 22 (see decision 30, §10). Courses new to this catalogue (first named in Model 5) are marked New.

IS-101 — Islamic Foundations. Core Aqeedah and Fiqh essentials for a learner starting from zero (Model 3's zero-prior-knowledge entry point). Outcome: recall essential Aqeedah and Fiqh accurately. Assessment: written examination.

IS-201 — Intermediate Aqeedah. Aqeedah taught as a connected, systematic body of knowledge rather than scattered facts. Outcome: explain Aqeedah systematically, not just recall it. Assessment: written examination.

IS-202 — Intermediate Fiqh. Systematic Fiqh instruction building on Foundation's essentials. Outcome: apply basic Fiqh rulings to everyday situations. Assessment: written examination.

IS-203 — Seerah I. The learner's first dedicated Seerah course, broken out from "Islamic foundations" (Model 4 §2). Outcome: recall and narrate the essential events of the Prophetic biography. Assessment: written examination.

IS-301 — Advanced Aqeedah. Deepens Aqeedah to independent, argument-following engagement. Outcome: reason independently within the Academy's manhaj on Aqeedah questions. Assessment: written examination.

IS-302 — Usul al-Fiqh. The methodology behind Fiqh rulings, not just the rulings themselves. Outcome: analyze how a Fiqh ruling is derived from its sources. Assessment: written examination.

IS-303 — Hadith Sciences. Hadith introduced and developed as its own dedicated discipline at Advanced tier. Outcome: explain Hadith classification and basic authentication criteria. Assessment: written examination.

IS-304 — Islamic Thought. Classical and contemporary Islamic intellectual tradition — kalam, philosophical theology, schools of thought — the Advanced-tier competency named in Model 3 §5, previously mis-housed under Islamic Civilization & Society before Model 5 corrected it. Outcome: engage the broader intellectual tradition, not only rulings. Assessment: written examination plus a short research essay.

IS-305 — Islamic Ethics. Akhlaq as a reasoned discipline — the theoretical counterpart to Tazkiyah's practiced formation. Outcome: reason about moral questions using Islamic ethical frameworks, not only rules. Assessment: written examination.

IS-401 — Comparative Fiqh. Comparative treatment of Fiqh positions across schools, the Diploma's mastery-tier Fiqh course. Outcome: compare and evaluate differing Fiqh positions on a given issue. Assessment: written examination.

IS-402 — Hadith Methodology (Takhrij). Hadith authentication methodology at mastery level. Outcome: trace and evaluate a Hadith's chain of transmission. Assessment: research project (a takhrij exercise).

IS-403 — Contemporary Islamic Issues. Fiqh- and Aqeedah-based reasoning applied to modern questions — bioethics, Islamic finance, technology — the juristic "can/should" question, distinct from Islamic Civilization & Society's sociological IC-401 (Model 5 §9). Outcome: apply juristic reasoning to a genuinely contemporary question. Assessment: written examination plus a case-study essay.

QS-101 — Qur'an Reading Foundations. Correct Qur'anic reading from a zero-prior-knowledge start. Outcome: read Qur'anic Arabic correctly at a foundational level. Assessment: oral recitation assessment.

QS-102 — Tajweed Foundations. Foundational Tajwid rules applied to correct recitation. Outcome: apply foundational Tajweed rules while reading. Assessment: oral recitation assessment.

QS-201 — Applied Tajweed. Tajwid applied with growing fluency across longer passages. Outcome: recite with improved fluency and Tajwid accuracy. Assessment: oral recitation assessment.

QS-401 — Tafsir II. Tafsir mastered at Diploma tier. Outcome: independently research and present a Tafsir analysis. Assessment: research project.

QS-402 — Ulum al-Qur'an. Sciences of the Qur'an — revelation circumstances, makki/madani, compilation history — the contextual scaffolding Tafsir depends on but does not itself teach (Model 5 §3). Outcome: explain how a verse's revelation context shapes its interpretation. Assessment: written examination.

AR-101 — Arabic Foundations. Zero-Arabic entry point building basic vocabulary and sentence structure. Outcome: recognize basic vocabulary and sentence structure. Assessment: written examination.

AR-201 — Arabic Grammar I. Grammar sufficient to reduce dependence on translation. Outcome: apply basic grammar rules to read simple texts. Assessment: written examination.

AR-301 — Arabic Grammar II. Grammar deepened toward reading primary texts directly. Outcome: read primary texts with reduced reliance on secondary summaries. Assessment: written examination.

AR-302 — Conversation. Spoken fluency and applied dialogue — the department's one topic that is not text-facing (Model 5 §4); needs only Intermediate grammar, not the full Advanced Grammar II, so it runs alongside AR-301 rather than after it. Outcome: hold a basic conversational exchange in Arabic. Assessment: oral/practical assessment.

AR-401 — Classical Arabic & Source Reading. Source-reading mastery, the proficiency level that makes direct source engagement possible. Outcome: read a classical source text directly, without translation support. Assessment: written examination.

AR-402 — Writing. Composition and written expression — distinct from grammar's rules and reading's comprehension (Model 5 §4). Outcome: compose a structured piece of Arabic writing on a given topic. Assessment: written composition assessment.

7. Course Quality Control

  • No unnecessary duplication. Confirmed against Model 5's seven-case audit, re-checked here with final codes assigned: IS-304/IC-301, IS-403/IC-401, and IE-405/IC-402 all read as close names but distinct disciplines by design; no new duplication was introduced by assigning codes. Full detail in §8.
  • Prerequisites are logical. Every prerequisite in §5 points to either the immediately preceding course in its own chain or a lower-tier grounding course — no forward references, no skipped tiers, no course listing a prerequisite from a level above it.
  • Advanced courses have sufficient preparation. Every Advanced-tier course requires at least one Intermediate-tier course, except AR-302 (Conversation), which intentionally requires only AR-201 rather than the full AR-301 chain, since conversational fluency does not depend on completing Advanced grammar first (Model 5 §4).
  • Courses belong to appropriate departments. Reconfirms Model 5 §9's resolutions now that real codes exist: IS-304 (Islamic Thought) sits under Islamic Studies, not Islamic Civilization & Society; IE-402 (Da'wah & Outreach) sits under Islamic Education & Tarbiyah, not Islamic Civilization & Society; IS-403/IC-401 and IE-405/IC-402 keep their department split by scope.
  • Programs contain enough relevant courses. 6 → 10 → 12 → 15 across Foundation → Intermediate → Advanced → Diploma (§4) — growing depth at every tier, no thin pathway.
  • No course exists only to fill space. Every course traces either to an explicit Model 1–4 requirement or a specific gap Model 5 named by discipline — none was added merely to make a department's course count match another's, which Model 2's Academic Design Rule and Model 4 §1's blockquote both rule out. Islamic Education & Tarbiyah's four new Diploma companions (IE-402–405) look like the largest single addition, but each answers a distinct, separately-named Model 5 gap (da'wah, curriculum design, youth education, family education), not one topic split four ways for volume.

9. Missing-Course Audit

  • No "Practical" type course exists. A genuine placement/practicum course — a teaching practicum for the teaching track, a da'wah practicum for IE-402, or a community-service placement tied to Model 1 Objective 10 — is absent from the catalogue. This is a real gap, not a naming oversight; a future model should decide whether to add one.
  • Specialized Certificate course lists don't exist yet. Model 3 §7 names candidate certificates and owning departments; Model 4 §2 defines the shape (3–5 core courses per certificate); no certificate has an actual course list. Still open.
  • Seerah has no Advanced or Diploma continuation. Only IS-203 exists; Model 5 decision 28 (is this deliberate, like Tazkiyah, or a genuine gap?) is still unresolved.
  • IE-402's placement is still provisional. Tier, prerequisite, and track are all open pending decision 32 (§10) — the catalogue-level restatement of Model 5 decision 26.
  • Hifz is not missing — it's a deliberate non-course. Model 5 §3 resolved this by tracking Hifz through the existing Qur'an reading/recitation chain and the Qur'an Memorization & Recitation cross-cutting unit rather than a standalone course. Listed here only to confirm it was considered, not overlooked.
